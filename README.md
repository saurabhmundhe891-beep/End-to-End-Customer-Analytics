# Customer Analytics | Excel, Python & Power BI

## About the Project

I built this project to explore customer data and understand what it can tell us about customer behaviour, churn, and revenue.

While working on the project, I used Excel for initial data analysis, Python for data cleaning and exploratory data analysis, and Power BI to build an interactive dashboard.

The main idea was not just to create charts, but to understand the patterns in the data and turn them into insights that could be useful for a business.

One of my findings was that the Basic subscription plan had a higher churn rate (24.83%) compared with the Standard plan (19.42%).

## What I Wanted to Understand

I explored a few questions throughout the project:

- How are customers distributed across different cities, age groups, and subscription plans?
- How many customers have stopped using the service?
- Are there any patterns in customer churn?
- Which cities and subscription plans contribute more to revenue?
- How does revenue change over time?
- What can customer engagement tell us about their behaviour?

## How I Worked on the Project

### 1. Exploring the Data with Excel

I started by opening the customer dataset in Excel to understand its structure and get a first look at the information.

I used Pivot Tables and Pivot Charts to summarize customer data and explore some initial patterns.

### 2. Data Cleaning and Preparation with Python

Next, I moved to Python and used Pandas and NumPy to prepare the data for analysis.

Some of the things I worked on included:

- Checking missing values and duplicate records.
- Identifying and handling invalid date records.
- Understanding data types and checking data consistency.
- Creating additional columns to support analysis.

### 3. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

After preparing the data, I explored it using Matplotlib and Seaborn.

I created different visualizations to understand customer demographics, engagement, churn, and revenue patterns.

This helped me look beyond individual records and identify patterns across customer groups.

### 4. Building the Power BI Dashboard

Finally, I imported the prepared dataset into Power BI and created a dashboard with three main pages.

**Customer Overview**
- Customer count and revenue KPIs.
- Customer distribution by city, gender, age group, and plan type.

**Customer Churn Analysis**
- Churned customer count and churn rate.
- Churn analysis across gender, age group, city, plan type, and marketing channel.

**Revenue Analysis**
- Total revenue and average revenue per customer.
- Revenue by city, subscription plan, gender, and marketing channel.
- Monthly revenue trend.

I also added slicers so users can filter the dashboard and explore different customer segments.

## Dataset Information

The dataset contains customer-level information such as:

- Customer ID, age, gender, and city.
- Signup date and last active date.
- Subscription plan and monthly fee.
- Tenure and support tickets.
- Average session time and discount percentage.
- Marketing channel and churn status.
- Total revenue.
